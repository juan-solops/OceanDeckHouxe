Diving in Abades: The Complete Guide to South Tenerife's Best Kept Underwater Secret

Updated May 2026 12 min read Local Diving Expert

Abades, a small fishing village in the municipality of Arico on South Tenerife's coast, has quietly earned a reputation as one of the Canary Islands' premier diving destinations. While tourists flock to the busy resorts of Los Cristianos and Playa de las Américas, experienced divers know that the real underwater magic happens here in South Tenerife's tranquil southeast corner.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about diving in Abades, South Tenerife — from the best dive sites and marine life encounters to practical information about dive centers, costs, and where to stay.

Why Abades is South Tenerife's Best Diving Location

Located approximately 25 kilometers from Tenerife South Airport (TFS), Abades sits on a dramatic stretch of South Tenerife's volcanic coastline. The village's position on the island's southeastern tip creates unique diving conditions that set it apart from other South Tenerife dive sites.

Exceptional Water Clarity

Thanks to South Tenerife's minimal rainfall and the volcanic rock formations around Abades, underwater visibility regularly exceeds 25-30 meters. During summer months, it's not uncommon to experience 40+ meter visibility — conditions that rival the Mediterranean's clearest waters.

The lack of sediment runoff in this part of South Tenerife means the water remains crystal clear year-round, making Abades ideal for underwater photography and videography.

Year-Round Diving Conditions

South Tenerife benefits from the Canary Islands' famous "eternal spring" climate. Water temperatures in Abades remain comfortable throughout the year:

This means you can dive comfortably in Abades, South Tenerife every single day of the year — a luxury few European dive destinations can offer.

Top Dive Sites in Abades, South Tenerife

The coastline around Abades and neighboring Arico offers diverse dive sites suitable for all experience levels. Here are the must-dive locations in this part of South Tenerife:

1. El Condesito (The Little Count)

Depth: 5-18 meters | Level: Beginner to Intermediate

El Condesito is Abades' most famous dive site and a South Tenerife classic. This shore dive features a dramatic volcanic reef system with numerous swim-throughs, caves, and overhangs. The site is named after a small wreck that rests at 12 meters.

What you'll see: Large schools of barracuda, moray eels, cuttlefish, octopus, angel sharks (occasionally), and colorful nudibranchs. The volcanic rock formations create a maze-like underwater landscape perfect for exploration.

💡 Local Tip: Visit El Condesito early morning for the best chance to spot angel sharks resting on the sandy patches between rocks. This critically endangered species is one of South Tenerife's most sought-after marine encounters.

2. La Cueva (The Cave)

Depth: 8-22 meters | Level: Intermediate

Located just south of Abades village, La Cueva is a spectacular cave dive that showcases South Tenerife's volcanic geology. The main cavern features stunning light effects as sunlight filters through cracks in the lava rock ceiling.

What you'll see: The cave itself is the star attraction, but you'll also encounter groupers, damselfish, and occasionally sea turtles cruising past the entrance.

3. Abades Wall (La Pared de Abades)

Depth: 12-30 meters | Level: Advanced

For experienced divers, Abades Wall offers one of South Tenerife's most dramatic underwater landscapes. This vertical volcanic wall drops from 12 meters to beyond recreational diving limits, creating a thrilling drift dive when conditions allow.

What you'll see: Pelagic species including tuna, amberjacks, and rays. The wall is covered in black coral, sponges, and sea fans. Lucky divers may spot manta rays gliding along the wall during summer months.

4. Palm Mar Wreck

Depth: 18-28 meters | Level: Advanced Open Water

While technically just outside Abades (15 minutes south along the South Tenerife coast), this intentionally sunk fishing vessel has become an artificial reef teeming with life. The wreck sits upright on a sandy bottom and is completely penetrable.

What you'll see: The wreck has attracted massive schools of fish, including barracuda, bream, and grunt. Moray eels have made homes in the ship's structure, and the surrounding area hosts stingrays and occasional angel sharks.

Marine Life in Abades, South Tenerife

The waters around Abades, South Tenerife support a rich diversity of Atlantic and Mediterranean marine species. The volcanic substrate and nutrient-rich currents create an ecosystem that supports everything from tiny nudibranchs to large pelagic visitors.

Common Species

  • Stingrays: Frequently seen gliding over sandy areas, especially early morning
  • Moray Eels: Green morays and Mediterranean morays inhabit every reef crevice
  • Barracuda: Large schools patrol the reefs, creating spectacular photo opportunities
  • Cuttlefish & Octopus: Master camouflage artists found on every dive
  • Parrotfish: Colorful and abundant, their "crunching" sounds fill the underwater soundscape
  • Damselfish & Chromis: Clouds of these small fish add color to the volcanic rocks
  • Groupers: Large dusky groupers are common, some reaching impressive sizes

Special Encounters

  • Angel Sharks: Abades is one of the few places in South Tenerife where you can still encounter these critically endangered sharks. They rest on sandy bottoms during the day.
  • Sea Turtles: Loggerhead turtles occasionally visit Abades' reefs, particularly in summer
  • Manta Rays: Rare but possible sightings during summer months along the deeper walls
  • Dolphins: Atlantic spotted dolphins and bottlenose dolphins sometimes approach divers in open water

🐢 Protecting South Tenerife's Marine Life

Abades is part of a protected marine area. Please follow these guidelines:

  • Never touch or harass marine life, especially angel sharks
  • Maintain excellent buoyancy to avoid damaging reefs
  • Don't feed fish or remove anything from the ocean
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen to protect South Tenerife's delicate ecosystem

Dive Centers in Abades, South Tenerife

Several professional dive centers operate in Abades and the surrounding South Tenerife area. Most offer multilingual instruction (English, German, Spanish, Dutch) and cater to divers of all levels.

What to Expect from Abades Dive Centers

  • PADI and SSI Certification: All major dive centers in South Tenerife offer internationally recognized certification courses
  • Small Group Sizes: Most centers limit groups to 4-6 divers per instructor
  • Equipment Rental: Full equipment rental available, including computers and cameras
  • Nitrox: Enriched air nitrox is available at most South Tenerife dive centers
  • Boat and Shore Dives: Options for both depending on conditions and preferences

When to Dive in Abades, South Tenerife

While diving is possible year-round in South Tenerife, certain seasons offer specific advantages:

Best Overall: April to October

Spring through autumn offers the warmest water temperatures (22-25°C) and best visibility in Abades. This is peak season for South Tenerife diving, but Abades remains far less crowded than other Canary Islands dive sites.

Best for Angel Sharks: November to March

Winter months in South Tenerife see increased angel shark activity in Abades. The water is slightly cooler (19-21°C), but you'll need only a 7mm wetsuit. Fewer tourists also mean more intimate dive experiences.

Best for Pelagic Species: June to September

Summer brings warmer currents to South Tenerife, attracting larger pelagic species to Abades. This is your best chance to encounter manta rays, large tuna, and even the occasional whale shark (very rare but possible).

Practical Information for Diving in Abades, South Tenerife

Getting to Abades

From Tenerife South Airport (TFS): 25-30 minutes by car via TF-1 motorway. Car rental is recommended for exploring South Tenerife.

From Tenerife North Airport (TFN): 60-75 minutes by car.

Public Transport: Limited bus service connects Abades to other South Tenerife towns, but schedules don't align well with diving activities.

What to Bring

  • Valid diving certification card
  • Dive logbook
  • Dive computer (or rent locally)
  • Underwater camera (South Tenerife's visibility makes photography spectacular)
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Light windbreaker for boat trips

Health & Safety

The nearest hyperbaric chamber is located in Santa Cruz de Tenerife (45 minutes from Abades). All reputable South Tenerife dive centers carry oxygen and first aid equipment. DAN (Divers Alert Network) insurance is recommended.
